# Scivias — Book Three: The History of Salvation

Book Three is the longest section, containing 13 visions that address the virtues, the building of the City of God, the last days, and the new heaven and earth. It culminates in the Symphony of the Blessed — where Hildegard's musical compositions are embedded within the visionary text.

## Vision 1: God and Man

> Then I looked, and behold, a tower of marvelous height and width, which was the wall of a celestial city. And it had no foundation, but rested on a living stone. And the living stone was the cornerstone of a great building.

---

## Vision 2: The Mystical Body

> And I saw the image of a woman from her waist up, of a pale color like the shimmering of the morning light. And her form was wrapped with a red glow, as a garment wraps one who puts it on.

---

## Vision 3: The Tower of the Church

> And I saw a tower made of stones of many colors — some white and some black, some ruddy and some yellowish — and each stone was polished, shining in its place.

---

## Vision 4: The Pillar of the Word of God

> I saw a great pillar the color of air, which was of marvelous height and breadth, and was full of eyes all around.

---

## Vision 5: The Zeal of God

> And I saw, as it were, a woman of great size. Her face was of such brightness that I could not fully behold it.

---

## Vision 10: The Son of Man

> Then I saw the Son of Man coming on the clouds with great glory, and all the angels with Him. And He separated the people gathered before Him into two groups, as a shepherd separates sheep from goats.

---

## Vision 11: The Last Days and the Fall of the Antichrist

> After this I saw an unclean beast of horrible and fierce appearance come from the North. It had scales of iron and bristles of fire, and its breath was a burning wind.

---

## Vision 12: The New Heaven and the New Earth

> And I saw a new heaven and a new earth. And I heard a voice saying: "Behold, all things are made new!"

---

## Vision 13: The Symphony of the Blessed

This final vision is unique in that it contains Hildegard's hymns and songs within the visionary framework. The blessed sing in harmony — the *symphonia* — praising God in a celestial concert that reflects the cosmic order. This vision provides the theological context for Hildegard's entire musical output.

> And I heard a sound like the voice of a multitude, singing in harmony praise to the celestial ranks. And this voice passed through me like the sound of many waters, like the voice of rushing thunder.
