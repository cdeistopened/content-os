## From the Symphonia armonie celestium revelationum

## O vis eternitatis — Responsory to God the Father

*The symbolic opening of the Symphonia. Though addressed to God the Father, the central theme is the Son's Incarnation.*

**Latin:**

> O vis eternitatis,
> que omnia ordinasti in corde tuo,
> per Verbum tuum omnia creata sunt
> sicut voluisti,
> et ipsum Verbum tuum
> induit carnem
> in formatione illa
> que educta est de Adam.
>
> Et sic indumenta ipsius
> a magno dolore
> abstersa sunt.
>
> O quam magna est benignitas Salvatoris
> qui omnia liberavit
> per incarnationem suam,
> quam divinitas exspiravit
> sine vinculo peccati.
>
> Et sic indumenta ipsius
> a magno dolore
> abstersa sunt.
>
> Gloria Patri et Filio
> et Spiritui sancto.
>
> Et sic indumenta ipsius
> a magno dolore
> abstersa sunt.

**English Translation:**

> O power of eternity,
> you ordered all things in your heart.
> Through your Word all things were created
> just as you willed,
> and your very Word
> put on flesh
> in that form
> which was drawn from Adam.
>
> And so his garments
> were cleansed
> of great sorrow.
>
> O how great is the kindness of the Savior
> who freed all things
> through his incarnation,
> which divinity breathed forth
> without the chain of sin.
>
> And so his garments
> were cleansed
> of great sorrow.

*Translation: Nathaniel M. Campbell, via the International Society of Hildegard von Bingen Studies (hildegard-society.org)*

## O virtus Sapientiae — Antiphon to Divine Wisdom

*A short antiphon celebrating Wisdom as a cosmic force with three wings.*

**Latin:**

> O virtus Sapientiae,
> quae circuiens circuisti
> comprehendendo omnia
> in una via, quae habet vitam,
> tres alas habens,
> quarum una in altum volat,
> et altera de terra sudat,
> et tertia undique volat.
> Laus tibi sit, sicut te decet,
> O Sapientia.

**English Translation:**

> O strength of Wisdom,
> who, circling, circled,
> enclosing all things
> in one path that has life,
> three wings you have:
> one soars to the heights,
> one distils its essence upon the earth,
> and the third is everywhere.
> Praise to you, as is fitting,
> O Wisdom.

## Ave generosa — Hymn to the Virgin

*One of Hildegard's most celebrated hymns. She weaves together her most characteristic images to celebrate the Virgin Mary's union with God and her giving birth to God's Son in the flesh.*

**Latin:**

> Ave generosa,
> gloriosa et intacta puella.
> Tu pupilla castitatis,
> tu materia sanctitatis,
> que Deo placuit.
>
> Nam hec superna infusio
> in te fuit,
> quod supernum Verbum
> in te carnem induit.
>
> Tu candidum lilium
> quod Deus ante omnem creaturam
> inspexit.
>
> O pulcherrima et dulcissima,
> quam valde Deus in te delectabatur,
> cum amplexionem caloris sui
> in te posuit,
> ita quod Filius eius
> de te lactatus est.
>
> Venter enim tuus gaudium habuit
> cum omnis celestis symphonia de te sonuit,
> quia virgo Filium Dei portasti,
> ubi castitas tua in Deo claruit.
>
> Viscera tua gaudium habuerunt
> sicut gramen super quod ros cadit
> cum ei viriditatem infudit,
> ut et in te factum est,
> o mater omnis gaudii.
>
> Nunc omnis Ecclesia in gaudio rutilet
> ac in symphonia sonet
> propter dulcissimam Virginem
> et laudabilem Mariam,
> Dei Genitricem. Amen.

**English Translation:**

> Hail, girl of a noble house,
> shimmering and unpolluted,
> you pupil in the eye of chastity,
> you essence of sanctity,
> who were pleasing to God.
>
> For the heavenly flood
> poured into you
> as the heavenly Word
> put on flesh in you.
>
> You are the lily, shining white,
> upon whom God gazed
> before all creation.
>
> O most beautiful and most sweet one,
> how greatly God delighted in you,
> when he set in you the embrace
> of his warmth,
> so that his Son
> was suckled by you.
>
> For your womb held joy
> when all the heavenly harmony rang out from you,
> because, O Virgin, you carried the Son of God,
> where your chastity blazed in God.
>
> Your flesh held joy
> like the grass upon which the dew falls,
> pouring its green life into it —
> and so it was in you,
> O mother of all joy.
>
> Now let all the Church blush with joy
> and resound in harmony
> for the sake of the sweetest Virgin
> and praiseworthy Mary,
> the Mother of God. Amen.

*Translation: Nathaniel M. Campbell, via the International Society of Hildegard von Bingen Studies*

## O viridissima virga — Song to the Virgin

*Hildegard elaborates the image of Mary as the branch of Jesse, evoking the symbolic state of viriditas, or "sacred aliveness."*

**Latin:**

> O viridissima virga, ave,
> que in ventoso flabro sciscitationis
> sanctorum prodisti.
>
> Cum venit tempus
> quod tu floruisti in ramis tuis,
> ave, ave fuit tibi,
> quia calor solis in te sudavit
> sicut odor balsami.
>
> Nam in te floruit pulcher flos
> qui odorem dedit
> omnibus aromatibus
> que arida erant.
>
> Et illa apparuerunt omnia
> in viriditate plena.
>
> Unde celi dederunt rorem super gramen
> et omnis terra leta facta est,
> quoniam viscera ipsius
> frumentum protulerunt
> et quoniam volucres celi
> nidos in ipsa habuerunt.
>
> Deinde facta est esca hominibus
> et gaudium magnum epulantium.
> Unde, o suavis Virgo,
> in te non deficit ullum gaudium.
>
> Hec omnia Eva contempsit.
>
> Nunc autem laus sit Altissimo.

**English Translation:**

> Hail, O greenest branch,
> you who came forth in the windy blast
> of the questioning of saints.
>
> When the time came
> that you blossomed in your branches,
> hail, hail to you,
> for the heat of the sun distilled in you
> a fragrance like balsam.
>
> For in you bloomed the beautiful flower
> that gave fragrance
> to all the spices
> that had been dry.
>
> And they all appeared
> in full greenness.
>
> Then the heavens gave dew upon the grass,
> and the whole earth was made glad,
> because her womb
> brought forth wheat,
> and because the birds of heaven
> had nests in her.
>
> Then food was made for humankind
> and great joy for the banqueters.
> And so, O sweet Virgin,
> in you no joy is lacking.
>
> All these things Eve scorned.
>
> But now let praise be to the Most High.

## O ignee Spiritus — Hymn to the Holy Spirit

*A hymn connecting the anointing oil of Confirmation with a medicinal balm used to heal wounds.*

**Latin:**

> O ignee Spiritus, laus tibi sit,
> qui in timpanis et citharis operaris.
>
> Mentes hominum de te flagrant
> et tabernacula animarum eorum
> vires ipsarum continent.
>
> Inde voluntas ascendit
> et gustum anime tribuit,
> et eius lucerna est desiderium.
>
> Intellectus te in dulcissimo sono
> advocat ac edificia tibi
> cum ratione parat,
> que in aureis operibus sudat.
>
> Tu autem semper gladium habes
> illud abscidere
> quod noxiale pomum
> per nigerrimum homicidium profert,
>
> quando nebula voluntatem
> et desideria contegit,
> in quibus anima volat
> et undique circuit.
>
> Sed et mens est cingulum
> voluntatis et anime.

**English Translation:**

> O fiery Spirit, praise to you,
> who work upon the timbrels and the lyres.
>
> Human minds blaze forth from you,
> and the tabernacles of their souls
> contain their powers.
>
> From this the will ascends
> and grants the soul its savor,
> and its lamp is desire.
>
> The intellect calls upon you
> with sweetest sound
> and prepares buildings for you
> with reason,
> which sweats in golden works.
>
> But you always hold the sword
> to cut off
> what the poisonous apple brings forth
> through the blackest murder,
>
> when a mist covers the will
> and the desires
> in which the soul flies about
> and circles everywhere.
>
> Yet the mind is the girdle
> of will and soul.

## O pastor animarum — Antiphon to Christ the Redeemer

**Latin:**

> O pastor animarum
> et o prima vox
> per quam omnes create sumus,
> nunc tibi, tibi placeat
> ut digneris nos liberare
> de miseriis et languoribus nostris.

**English Translation:**

> O Shepherd of our souls,
> and O first voice
> through which we were all created,
> now may it please you,
> please you,
> to deign to free us
> from our miseries and our weaknesses.

## Laus Trinitati — Antiphon to the Trinity

**Latin:**

> Laus Trinitati,
> que sonus et vita
> ac creatrix omnium est
> in vita ipsorum,
> et que laus angelorum
> et mirus splendor arcanorum,
> que hominibus ignota sunt, est,
> et que in omnibus vita est.

**English Translation:**

> Praise to the Trinity,
> who is sound and life
> and creator of all things
> in the life of them all,
> and who is the praise of angels
> and the wondrous splendor of mysteries
> unknown to humankind,
> and who is life in all things.
