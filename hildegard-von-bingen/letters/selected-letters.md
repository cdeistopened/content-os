# Selected Letters of Hildegard von Bingen

Hildegard wrote nearly 400 letters to popes, emperors, kings, bishops, abbots, abbesses, monks, and nuns. Her correspondence reveals her as "determined reformer, castigating seer, theoretical musician, patient adviser, and exorcist."

---

## To Bernard of Clairvaux (c. 1146–1147)

*Sick with anxiety about her revelations, Hildegard writes to the most influential churchman of the age, seeking his approval to write publicly.*

> O venerable father Bernard, I lay my claim before you, for, highly honored by God, you bring fear to the immoral foolishness of this world and, in your intense zeal and burning love for the Son of God, gather men into Christ's army to fight under the banner of the cross against pagan savagery.
>
> I beseech you in the name of the Living God to give heed to my queries. Father, I am greatly disturbed by a vision which has appeared to me through divine revelation, a vision seen not with my fleshly eyes but only in my spirit.
>
> Wretched, and indeed more than wretched in my womanly condition, I have from earliest childhood seen great marvels which my tongue has no power to express but which the Spirit of God has taught me that I may believe.
>
> Steadfast and gentle father, in your kindness respond to me, your unworthy servant, who has never, from her earliest childhood, lived one hour free from anxiety.
>
> More than two years ago, indeed, I saw you in a vision, like a man looking straight into the sun, bold and unafraid. And I wept, because I myself am so timid and fearful.

*Bernard replied encouragingly, urging her to recognize her gift as grace and to respond with humility and devotion.*

**Source:** Translation by Joseph L. Baird and Radd K. Ehrman, *The Letters of Hildegard of Bingen*, Vol. 1, Oxford University Press, 1994. Excerpts via Epistolae (epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu).

## Hildegard's Declaration of Her Visionary Experience

*From the preface to Scivias, in which Hildegard describes the nature of her visions:*

> It happened that, in the eleven hundred and forty-first year of the Incarnation of the Son of God, Jesus Christ, when I was forty-two years and seven months old, Heaven was opened and a fiery light of exceeding brilliance came and permeated my whole brain, and inflamed my whole heart and my whole breast, not like a burning but like a warming flame, as the sun warms anything its rays touch. And immediately I knew the meaning of the exposition of the Scriptures.
>
> But I had no perception of the form of any human being, and I saw all this not with the external eyes or through the thoughts of my heart, nor through any combination of my five senses, but only in my soul, with my external eyes open, and so I never fell prey to ecstasy in the visions, but I kept seeing them while awake, both day and night.

## To Pope Eugenius III (c. 1148)

*After receiving papal approval at the Synod of Trier, Hildegard continued to correspond with Eugenius:*

> O gentle father, in a true vision I have been instructed to present these words to you: the mighty and immovable God stretches the firmament in the right disposition of His works, and nothing that stands against Him can prevail.

---

## To Emperor Frederick Barbarossa (c. 1152–1170)

*Hildegard corresponded with Frederick Barbarossa over many years, at first with encouragement, later with increasingly sharp prophetic warnings as he supported antipopes against the legitimate papacy.*

> He Who Is says: I destroy disobedience and the resistance of those who scorn Me. Woe, woe to the malice of wicked men who turn from Me! Hear this, O king, if you wish to live; otherwise My sword will smite you!

---

## To the Prelates of Mainz (1178–1179)

*Hildegard's last great battle. At age 79, she allowed the burial of an excommunicated man in her convent cemetery, claiming he had received last rites. The prelates of Mainz placed the Rupertsberg under interdict, forbidding the nuns from singing the Divine Office — a devastating punishment for a community whose life centered on music.*

*Hildegard wrote a passionate defense not only of the burial but of sacred music itself:*

> Those who choose silence regarding the praises owed to God without just cause will lose their place among the chorus of angels, unless they have made amends through true penitence and humble compensation.
>
> Consider, too, that just as the body of Jesus Christ was born of the purity of the Virgin Mary through the operation of the Holy Spirit, so the canticle of praise, reflecting celestial harmony, is rooted in the Church through the Holy Spirit. The body is the vestment of the spirit, which has a living voice, and so it is proper for the body, in harmony with the soul, to use its voice to sing praises to God.

*The interdict was eventually lifted shortly before Hildegard's death.*

## Key Correspondents

Hildegard's letters were addressed to figures including:

- **Pope Eugenius III** — who authorized her visions
- **Pope Anastasius IV**
- **Pope Adrian IV**
- **Pope Alexander III**
- **Emperor Frederick Barbarossa**
- **King Henry II of England**
- **Eleanor of Aquitaine**
- **Bernard of Clairvaux**
- **Abbot Suger of Saint-Denis**
- Various bishops, abbots, abbesses, monks, and nuns across the Holy Roman Empire
