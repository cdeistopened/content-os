# Scientific & Medical Writings

Hildegard's scientific works are among the earliest natural history and medical texts from the German-speaking world. While the original Latin texts are public domain, complete English translations remain under copyright.

---

## Physica (Natural History)

*Full title: Liber Subtilitatum Diversarum Naturarum Creaturarum* — "Book of the Subtleties of the Diverse Qualities of Created Things"

The *Physica* is a comprehensive natural history organized into nine books:

1. **Plants** (*De Plantis*) — 230 chapters on herbs, grains, and other plants
2. **Elements** (*De Elementis*) — on the four elements and their properties
3. **Trees** (*De Arboribus*) — 63 chapters on trees and their uses
4. **Stones** (*De Lapidibus*) — 26 chapters on minerals and gems
5. **Fish** (*De Piscibus*) — 36 chapters on fish and aquatic creatures
6. **Birds** (*De Avibus*) — 72 chapters on birds
7. **Animals** (*De Animalibus*) — 45 chapters on land animals
8. **Reptiles** (*De Reptilibus*) — 18 chapters on reptiles and small creatures
9. **Metals** (*De Metallis*) — 8 chapters on metals

For each entry, Hildegard describes the item's basic qualities — whether hot or cold, dry or moist — then elaborates on its medicinal value and explains how to prepare and apply medications.

### Notable Entries

Hildegard's plant descriptions include many species still used in herbal medicine today. She wrote extensively about spelt (which she considered the ideal grain), fennel, galangal, and many others. Her approach combined empirical observation with her theological framework of *viriditas*.

## Causae et Curae (Causes and Cures)

*Full title: Liber Compositae Medicinae* — "Book of Compound Medicine"

Where *Physica* catalogs natural substances, *Causae et Curae* is a medical compendium focused on disease and treatment. It addresses:

- **Cosmology and creation** — the origin of the universe and humanity
- **Human physiology** — the four humors and temperaments
- **Disease** — causes of illness, both physical and spiritual
- **Treatment** — remedies using herbs, stones, and other natural substances
- **Diet and nutrition** — Hildegard's dietary recommendations
- **Women's health** — menstruation, pregnancy, and childbirth
- **Psychology** — emotional and mental health

Hildegard's medical approach is distinctive for its integration of body and soul. She saw illness as a disruption of *viriditas* — the body's greening life-force — and treatment as its restoration.

## Lingua Ignota (Unknown Language)

Though not strictly a scientific work, Hildegard's *Lingua Ignota* deserves mention as the earliest known constructed language in history. It consists of over 1,000 words with a glossary providing Latin and German equivalents, plus an alternative alphabet of 23 letters (*Litterae Ignotae*).

The purpose of the language remains debated — it may have been a mystical language for private devotion, a secret communication system for the convent, or a theological exercise in naming creation.
