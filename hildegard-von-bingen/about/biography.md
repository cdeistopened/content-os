# Hildegard von Bingen (1098–1179)

> "I, a poor little figure of a woman, saw a great mountain the color of iron, and enthroned on it One of such great glory that it blinded my sight."

## Life

**Born:** 1098, Bermersheim vor der Höhe, County Palatine of the Rhine, Holy Roman Empire
**Died:** September 17, 1179, Rupertsberg monastery, near Bingen am Rhein
**Venerated:** Canonized May 10, 2012, by Pope Benedict XVI
**Title:** Doctor of the Church (declared October 7, 2012)
**Feast Day:** September 17

Hildegard was the tenth child of a noble family. At age eight, she was offered as an oblate to the Benedictine monastery at Disibodenberg, where she was educated by the anchoress Jutta von Sponheim. When Jutta died in 1136, Hildegard was elected magistra (leader) of the small community of nuns.

From the age of three, Hildegard experienced visions — what she called "the Shade of the Living Light" — a constant inner luminosity that she described as seeing "in my spirit and not with external eyes." She kept these experiences private for decades.

At age 42, she received a divine command: **"Write down what you see and hear."** With the encouragement of her confessor and the monk Volmar, she began writing *Scivias*, which took ten years to complete (1141–1151). Pope Eugenius III, at the urging of Bernard of Clairvaux, read excerpts at the Synod of Trier in 1147–1148 and authorized Hildegard to continue writing.

Between 1147 and 1150, Hildegard founded a new monastery on the Rupertsberg, near Bingen, overcoming significant resistance. She later founded a second monastery at Eibingen, across the Rhine.

## Works

### Visionary Trilogy
- **Scivias** (*Sci vias Domini* — "Know the Ways of the Lord"), 1141–1151 — 26 visions on creation, redemption, and the Church
- **Liber Vitae Meritorum** ("Book of Life's Merits"), 1158–1163 — 35 visions on virtues and vices
- **Liber Divinorum Operum** ("Book of Divine Works"), 1163–1174 — 10 visions on the cosmos and humanity

### Music
- **Symphonia armonie celestium revelationum** ("Symphony of the Harmony of Celestial Revelations") — 77 liturgical compositions
- **Ordo Virtutum** ("Order of the Virtues") — the earliest surviving morality play

### Scientific & Medical
- **Physica** ("Natural History") — 9 books on plants, elements, trees, stones, fish, birds, animals, reptiles, and metals
- **Causae et Curae** ("Causes and Cures") — a medical compendium on disease and treatment

### Letters
- Nearly 400 letters to popes, emperors, kings, bishops, abbots, abbesses, monks, and nuns — including correspondence with Pope Eugenius III, Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, King Henry II of England, Eleanor of Aquitaine, and Bernard of Clairvaux

### Other
- **Lingua Ignota** ("Unknown Language") — a constructed language of over 1,000 words, the earliest known constructed language in history
- **Litterae Ignotae** — an alternative alphabet of 23 letters
- Extensive theological and philosophical writings

## Key Concepts

### Viriditas (Greening Power)
Hildegard's most distinctive concept. *Viriditas* represents the divine life-force — the "green" vitality that sustains all living things. She connected it to spiritual health, creativity, and the Holy Spirit's presence in creation. Its opposite, *ariditas* (dryness), represented sin and spiritual death.

### The Living Light
Hildegard described her visionary experiences as encounters with "the Living Light" (*lux vivens*) — a constant, non-ecstatic illumination she experienced from childhood. Unlike other mystics, she remained fully conscious during her visions.

### Cosmic Egg
In her cosmological visions, Hildegard depicted the universe as an egg-shaped structure with humanity at the center, connected to all elements of creation — anticipating ecological thought by centuries.

## Legacy

Hildegard was one of the most remarkable figures of the medieval world:

- **The most recorded medieval composer** — her music continues to be performed and recorded worldwide
- **A pioneer of natural history** — her scientific works are among the earliest to describe medicinal plants and minerals in German-speaking lands
- **A theological authority** — she preached publicly at a time when women were forbidden from doing so, and corresponded as an equal with the most powerful figures of her age
- **A visionary artist** — the illuminations accompanying her visions are among the most striking images of medieval art
- **An ecological thinker** — her concept of *viriditas* and interconnected creation resonates strongly with modern environmental thought

She died on September 17, 1179, at the age of 81. Reports at the time described two arcs of light appearing in the sky over her room at the moment of her death.
